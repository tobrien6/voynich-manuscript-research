# Dee evidence: money and a recalled book in printed editions

This follows the [provenance audit](DEE_EVIDENCE.md), which left two cited
printed passages uninspected. The [new ledger](dee-printed-testimony.json) pins
both retrieved scans, publication metadata, exact page mappings and evidential
limits. The earlier ledger remains a record of its original inspection scope.

## Printed money memorandum

The [1659 Casaubon edition](https://archive.org/details/truefaithfulrela00deej),
contributed to the public archive by Getty Research Institute, has the inspected
imprint London, D. Maxwell for T. Garthwait. Printed pages **447–448** correspond
to PDF pages **441–442** in this 498-page file. The title is PDF page 9.

The Latin memorandum begins near the foot of p. 447, dated **17 October 1586
as printed**. It names Pucci and discusses disputed money, accusations and a
settlement. The short anchor `630 Ducatos` appears alongside an 800-florin
amount. The continuation at the top of p. 448 describes witnesses, a signed
acknowledgement and an 800-florin settlement, with additional amounts described
as 2,000 ducats and more than 400 `Doleri` on the table. These are different
amounts and denominations, not a currency conversion or a sum inferred here.
The 1587 action heading lower on p. 448 starts another entry.

**Inference from the inspected context:** the 630-ducat mention belongs to this
money-dispute narrative. It does not by itself identify a book, document a
Voynich sale, or trace those funds to a 600-ducat manuscript payment. This does
not prove that such a transaction never occurred or explain every coin’s origin.
The note supplies no full Latin translation, calendar conversion or autograph
inspection. Casaubon’s title-page assertion of an original written by Dee is a
publication claim, not independent authentication of the manuscript source.

## Printed recollection

The [1852 Wilkin edition, Volume III](https://archive.org/details/worksbro03browuoft),
contributed by Robarts Library, University of Toronto, has the inspected imprint
London, Henry G. Bohn. Printed pages **530–531** are PDF pages **542–543** in
this 568-page file; the title is PDF page 7.

Under the heading of Browne’s letter to Elias Ashmole, Browne recounts Arthur
Dee’s statements about a book found beside alchemical powder. The book is
reported as `containing nothing butt hieroglyphicks`; Browne says he did not
hear that John Dee could understand it. The narrative continues on p. 531 and
ends with the edition’s `(No Signature.)` before a separate letter to John Aubrey.
The running heads show 1674 and 1672–3. They are not an exact dateline for this
letter, and this note assigns no letter date from them.

**Transmission:** Arthur’s reported recollections → Browne’s recollection of
those conversations → Wilkin’s printed representation → the inspected scan.
This is not Browne’s direct inspection of an authenticated Voynich manuscript.
The passage supplies no unique object identifier or physical comparison adequate
to establish that the recalled book is MS 408. Its historical wording does not
identify Egyptian hieroglyphs or another script, and its alchemical claims are
not adopted as independently observed events. The original letter remains
uninspected; the note neither proves nor excludes ownership of MS 408.

## Inspection and next test

Inspected 2026-10-12. The two title pages and four complete passage pages were
rendered and visually checked. Downloaded PDF lengths and SHA-256 hashes are in
the ledger; both files also match the Archive metadata’s SHA-1 checksums.
OCR missed the relevant
630-ducat occurrence and did not reliably locate the printed Casaubon page
numbers. Search misses were not treated as absence. These mappings apply to
these exact scans, not other editions or manuscript foliation.

The contribution advances the two printed-passage follow-ups while retaining
all unresolved original-document and object-identity questions. Compare the
original letter and memorandum with their edited representations before a
stronger attribution or transaction claim. No folio alias, corpus input, glyph
reading or decipherment hypothesis changes. Source PDFs, OCR, extracted text
and rendered pages remain local; only citation metadata and original observations
are published.
