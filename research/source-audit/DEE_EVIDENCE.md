Inspected 2026-10-12, America/New_York. This follows the source audit's
[ownership question](README.md#1-john-dee-ownership-is-disputed-within-yales-sources).
The [documentary ledger](dee-evidence.json) preserves source identities,
reference spellings, mediated quotations, access limits and unresolved tests.
It establishes a citation trail, not an owner, foliator or cipher author.

The ownership sentence in the current overview does not name the research
behind its doubt or discuss the numeral comparison. This audit cannot identify
the 2016 study as that sentence's source. Nor does the study's stronger negative
judgment automatically establish the opposite hand attribution. Specimens,
their identities, selection and comparative method still need review.

The second row is a quotation in an author's research note, not an inspected
image of the 1990 printed entry. The differing digits are directly observable
in these two representations. This does **not** verify the original book's
wording, show which reference is correct, or establish a catalog typo. Keep
both spellings and their documentary paths; do not silently substitute one.

The separate [catalog-reference follow-up](DEE_CATALOG_REFERENCES.md) identifies
487 and 847 as distinct catalog targets and records 1790’s composite context.
It leaves the original wording and manuscript comparison unresolved.

Before a numeral comparison, inspect the original entry and authenticate the
specified Bodleian leaves. A shelfmark, a leaf number, a printed page, a Voynich
folio label and an inferred writing hand are separate identifiers. Neither
`487` nor `847` is a Voynich foliation offset or a new alias. No image or native
transcription identifier changes as a result of this audit.

## What the cited letter can support

The older catalog's supplementary item A reproduces Marci's letter. Its purchase
report passes from Raphael to Marci; the carrier who received 600 ducats is not
named there as Dee. This is an observation of the catalog's Latin reproduction,
not a fresh inspection of the autograph. Preserve this evidential path instead
of converting a reported purchase into a directly documented Dee sale.

The 2016 study's printed pp. 310-312 also discuss the recalled book and money
arguments. Their source trail includes Wilkin (1852), pp. 530-531, and Casaubon
(1659), pp. 447-448. Those editions were **not** directly inspected in this
contribution. The ledger retains the follow-up references without adopting
their historical interpretations or supplying new translations.

## Access and reproduction limits

The author-hosted study PDF has 108 pages, including other material; printed
pp. 310-312 are PDF pages 20-22 (one-based). All three complete pages were
rendered and inspected, including footnotes 21-29. Reading the text layer alone
would miss the distinction between publication pagination and file sequence.

The Bibliographical Society's [November 2009 corrections](https://bibsoc.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2013/01/John-Dees-Library-Catalogue-4.pdf)
and [new-series addendum](https://bibsoc.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2013/01/John-Dees-library-catalogue-2019-03-15.pdf)
have 25 and four PDF pages. Their headings were visually checked. Extracted
text has no literal whole-word `Voynich`, `Beinecke`, `408`, or `DM93` matches.
This narrow search does not prove there are no relevant corrections elsewhere,
that an original attribution survives unchanged, or that all textual variants
have been found. These files are supplements, not the original printed entry.
The new-series filename includes a date; its first page supplies no dated
edition statement, so the ledger does not promote that filename to one.

The original 1990 entry and comparative Bodleian images remain uninspected.
A Philip Neal excerpt endpoint was also sought, but direct retrieval failed
certificate verification and the web open returned no readable content. It is
an access lead, not evidence substituted for the original book. No credentials
or nonpublic archival material were used, and no institution was contacted.

The ledger fingerprints the six inspected response bodies. It stores metadata,
short original summaries and references; source PDFs, HTML, extracted text and
rendered pages stay local. A changed endpoint or digest requires a new source
inspection. The hash of a web copy cannot authenticate an autograph or a
handwriting attribution.
