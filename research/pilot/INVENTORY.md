# Ten-folio pilot inventory

Inspected on **2026-10-11** (America/New_York). This contribution supplies ten source-linked observation records for the project's first inventory task. It establishes no transcription, statistical result, or decipherment.

## Question and method

Can a small sample describe text placement and illustration forms across Yale's six catalog groups without treating catalog descriptions as readings of the text?

The [selection protocol](SAMPLING.md) was present in the local project before this run inspected the selected images. It chooses the first two available exactly labeled rectos in Parts I, II, III, and V, the first in Part VI, and the complete `85v and 86r (foldout)` image in Part IV. The targets are 1r, 2r, 67r, 68r, 75r, 76r, 85v, 87r, 88r, and 103r. No target was replaced after inspection.

Each target is represented by one Yale IIIF canvas. These are **ten target folio sides and ten image records**, not ten isolated images of independent leaves. The 85v record covers the shared foldout with 86r. The images labeled 67r and 68r also contain multiple drawn panels. The additional panels are retained as context within their canvas record.

Descriptions below are direct observations of JPEG derivatives bounded by 1,800 × 1,800 pixels. Catalog groupings are reported separately from [S002](../SOURCES.md). [S003](https://collections.library.yale.edu/catalog/2002046) and [S004](https://collections.library.yale.edu/manifests/2002046) supply image labels and identifiers. All ten derivatives were visually inspected; fine glyphs, exact line counts, plant identification, and image segmentation were outside scope.

[Machine-readable records](inventory.json) retain exact labels, persistent catalog image links, canvas URIs, full and inspected image URLs, original and derivative dimensions, inspection date, SHA-256 checksums, observations, and uncertainty fields. No source images are redistributed.

## Result and limitations

The pilot captures markedly different layouts: text blocks around plants (2r, 87r), circular/radial writing (67r, 68r, the shared 85v/86r foldout), dense writing around figures (75r), dense writing without a large illustration (76r), rows of roots with vessels (88r), and writing beside marginal stars (103r). These descriptions support choosing layout-specific transcription units; they do not establish what the text means.

The observations challenge two convenient assumptions. An exactly labeled recto canvas can contain several foldout panels (67r, 68r), and a catalog section is not visually uniform: Part III includes the selected text-dense 76r, while Part V includes both a large whole plant on 87r and detached forms with vessels on 88r. Preserve the catalog grouping and the observed content as separate fields.

Allocation is 2, 2, 2, 1, 2, and 1 records across Parts I–VI. Selection favors section beginnings and rectos. It omits most versos, interior and end-of-section examples, partial scans, and missing-leaf analysis. Shared panels are dependent. No prevalence estimates or manuscript-wide generalizations can be made from this sample. The observations have not received an independent second review; reduced image scale especially limits small foldout lettering. Image checksums identify the inspected JPEG bytes but do not guarantee future IIIF derivatives remain byte-identical.

## Reproduce the source checks

From the repository root, using Python 3 and curl:

```sh
curl -fsSL https://collections.library.yale.edu/manifests/2002046 -o /tmp/voynich-manifest.json
python3 scripts/verify_pilot.py --manifest /tmp/voynich-manifest.json
```

The script recomputes the label-based selection and checks the ten image identifiers, persistent links, canvas URIs, image URLs, dimensions, and required observation fields. It reports whether the manifest's byte checksum matches the recorded version. It cannot verify the visual descriptions. Reopen each linked image for that step; `inspection_image_url` in the JSON provides the derivative used here.
